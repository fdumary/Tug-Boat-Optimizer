# QAOA angle tuning: CVaR objective and multi-start (2026-10-17)

Simulator only. Problem: 4 tugs, 4 ships (2 contract, 2 spot), 18 qubits. Baseline is `main` at `7365650`.

## What changed

1. **CVaR objective** (`QAOASimulator.cvar_value`, `cvar_alpha=0.1`). COBYLA now tunes γ, β to minimise
   the mean energy of the best 10% of outcomes, instead of the average energy over all outcomes.
   The average is dominated by heavily penalised invalid schedules; QAOA only needs the best shots
   to be good (Barkoutsos et al., "Improving Variational Quantum Optimization using CVaR", Quantum 4, 256, 2020).
2. **Multi-start** (`optimize_parameters(n_starts=8)`). COBYLA runs from 8 random starting angles,
   and the best result is kept. A single start can get stuck in a poor local minimum.
3. **Fast simulator** (`QAOASimulator.statevector_array`). It applies the cost layer as
   `exp(-iγ·diag_E)` and the mixer as per-qubit RX directly on a numpy vector. This was needed to
   make 8 starts affordable (about 0.07 s per evaluation vs about 0.5 s through Qiskit at 18 qubits).
   It matches Qiskit's `Statevector` to within 2e-15 at p=1 and p=2. It is used up to 20 qubits;
   above that the code falls back to Qiskit and the plain expectation value.

New defaults in `scripts/run_quantum_optimizer.py`: `--cvar-alpha 0.1 --starts 8`.
`--cvar-alpha 1.0 --starts 1` gives the old behaviour.

## Results (`scripts/run_qaoa_benchmark.py`, `results/`)

| Scenario | Tuning | Chance per shot of an optimal schedule | Chance a 4,000-shot run finds it | Approx. ratio | Tuning time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm, p=1 | baseline | 0.000% | 0.8% | 0.79 | 2 s |
| Calm, p=1 | CVaR + 8 starts | **3.46%** | 100% | 0.96 | 22 s |
| Calm, p=2 | baseline | 2.94% | 100% | 0.93 | 5 s |
| Calm, p=2 | CVaR + 8 starts | **6.35%** | 100% | 0.93 | 80 s |
| Windy, p=1 | baseline | 0.001% | 3.2% | 0.84 | 2 s |
| Windy, p=1 | CVaR + 8 starts | **0.52%** | 100% | 0.92 | 19 s |
| Windy, p=2 | baseline | 0.003% | 11.8% | 0.83 | 4 s |
| Windy, p=2 | CVaR + 8 starts | **1.31%** | 100% | 0.95 | 78 s |

Random guessing: 0.009% (calm), 0.005% (windy).

End to end, `run_quantum_optimizer.py --tugs 4 --no-qec` now returns the optimum in both calm
(−9,000) and windy (+1,500) **without** the classical repair step.

## Which change did the work (`cvar_sweep.py`, same instance, 100 COBYLA iterations, seed 123)

| | Calm p=1 | Calm p=2 | Windy p=1 | Windy p=2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| expectation, 1 start | 0.00% | 2.69% | 0.001% | 0.003% |
| expectation, 8 starts | 3.02% | 6.10% | 0.29% | 1.43% |
| CVaR 0.25, 8 starts | 3.38% | 6.28% | 0.50% | 1.46% |
| **CVaR 0.10, 8 starts** | 3.46% | 6.35% | 0.52% | 1.31% |
| CVaR 0.05, 8 starts | 3.46% | 5.15% | 0.52% | 0.90% |
| CVaR 0.01, 8 starts | 1.13% | 3.95% | 0.55% | 1.42% |

Multi-start gives most of the gain. CVaR adds a further 10-80% at p=1 and is roughly neutral at p=2.
α = 0.1 was picked as the best all-round value; α = 0.01 is too greedy.

## Caveats
- One instance (4 tugs), simulator only, no noise. On hardware, CVaR would be computed from shots,
  and tuning would be done in the simulator first, as now.
- Tuning is about 10-15× slower (8 starts × 100 iterations), still under 90 s at p=2.

## Reproduce
```
pip install qiskit qiskit-ibm-runtime dimod scipy matplotlib
python scripts/run_qaoa_benchmark.py            # ~5 min, writes results/
```
